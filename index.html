The Problem

Why WA growers are switching to spray drones.

Four problems we hear on almost every call. A spray drone fixes all of them.

Wet ground stops the tractor.

Soggy paddocks close your spray window. A drone flies regardless of how wet the ground is, so the crop gets treated on time.

Skilled spray operators are scarce.

Experienced operators are hard to find on short notice. One trained drone pilot covers the workload of two or three traditional operators.

Drift complaints cost you the next job.

Targeted application, GPS-locked passes and lower product volumes give you a cleaner compliance position. And a cleaner phone call with the neighbour.

Peak season is shorter every year.

The spring window narrows when conditions shift. A drone stretches your billable days at both ends of the season.

Questions

Questions buyers ask in the research phase.

Depends on your hectares and your appetite for owning kit. Below about 800 ha a season, a contractor is usually the right call. Above that, and especially if you have a recurring spray window every year, owning makes sense within two to three seasons. We'll do this maths with you on a callback.
T100 if you're contracting or running mixed broadacre at scale. T25 P if you're a vineyard, orchard, or under about 1,500 ha. If you're crossing both worlds, broadacre and specialty, most of our buyers run a T100 and add a T25 P in year two. Schedule a callback and we'll work it through against your actual operation.

Commercial agricultural use requires a Remote Pilot Licence (RePL) and a Part 137 certification for chemical application. We don't run these courses ourselves, but we work alongside WA-based CASA-credentialed training providers and we'll connect you with the right one for your situation.

What we include in every Agras package is on-day setup, a hands-on demo, and operator walkthrough so you're flying confidently from delivery.
